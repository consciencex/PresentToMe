02 How it works

Three times the battery needs the grid.

1 · Winter

A long winter evening

Heating and lights run longer. The grid covers what is over.

2 · Cloud

A day of thick cloud

Too little sun to fill it. The grid runs the evening.

3 · Power

Oven and car at once

It gives only so much at once. Above that, the grid joins in.

Example data. The winter evening, the cloudy day and the two appliances are illustrative. The lines for capacity, one evening and power come from the deck model.

The limits

A battery is a tank, and a tank has edges. Three times it needs the grid.

One, a long winter evening. Heating and lights run for more hours, and the evening takes more than the battery holds. The part above the line comes from the grid.

Two, a day of thick cloud. The line is one evening. On a clear day the roof has far more than that to spare. Under thick cloud it has a fraction, so the battery starts the evening nearly empty.

Three, power at one moment. A usual evening is far below the line. Turn the oven on while the car is charging, and the house asks for more than twice what the battery can give. The grid joins in and nothing switches off. [If asked, the control board sets this limit. A bigger battery of the same kind does not raise it.] Those are the edges for the example house.
