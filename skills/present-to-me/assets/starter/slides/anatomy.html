Example data. Harbor Foods is invented. System size from the model at $0.90 a watt; the inverter and battery sizes are typical values for a system this size, not quotes.

Four parts

Here is what we would actually be buying. The whole system is four parts, and the building does not change.

First, the panels. Rows of them across the whole roof, 800 kilowatts at peak. They turn daylight into direct current.

Second, the inverters, three cabinets on the end wall. They turn that direct current into the mains power our compressors and lights run on.

Third, a battery the size of a shipping container. It is not there to store the afternoon for the night. It rides through a passing cloud or a short outage, so the cold rooms stay cold.

Last, the grid meter. We stay on the grid. On a sunny day the roof makes about a quarter of what we use, and the grid brings the rest, day and night. Four parts, and only the panels are on the roof.
