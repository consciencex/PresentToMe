Three ways to pay

This chart compares one thing only: the power the roof could supply, about a quarter of what we use. The rest comes from the grid whichever way we choose, so it is left out. A, in blue, is today. We pay for every unit, and the line climbs for as long as we operate.

B, in purple, is renting. A supplier puts panels on our roof at their cost and sells us the power a little below the grid price. No money down, and the line is a bit lower. But it is still a line that climbs forever.

C, in orange, is owning. It starts high, because we pay for the system on day one. Then it is almost flat: cleaning and monitoring. The first ring is where owning has cost less than the grid. The second is where it has cost less than renting. The bands under the chart say which option is cheapest in each period: renting first, then owning for the rest of the system's life.
