A sunny day

Here is one sunny weekday, hour by hour.

The black line is what the site draws. It never drops much at night, because the cold rooms run all the time, and it rises through the working day. Today all of it comes from the grid.

Now add the roof. The orange is what the panels deliver. Through the middle of the day it carries most of the load, and over the whole day it supplies this share of our energy. [If asked: this is a sunny day; the yearly average share is lower, and the money model uses the yearly figure.]

So the roof takes the expensive midday hours, including the peak that sets our demand charge. Evenings and nights stay on the grid. Next: what that is worth in money.
