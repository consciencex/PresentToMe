Three things we don't control

Our power bill is the second-biggest cost in this building, and three things about it are decided by someone else.

First, price. We choose how much we use, but the grid sets what each unit costs. The bill has climbed every year for five years. At the rise the utility expects, today's monthly bill keeps climbing for five more years. [If asked: the history is an example series; the forward line uses the model's growth rate.]

Second, the peak. Most hours we draw well below our busiest hour, but the demand charge is set by that one busy hour. The compressors all kick in around midday, and we pay for that hour all month.

Third, outages. The grid decides when it fails, not us. In a three-hour cut the cold room warms past its limit, and every pallet in it is at risk. Next: what can we do about these three?
